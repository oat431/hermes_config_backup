# Thai Career and Technology Curriculum Research Pattern

Use this reference when building a Thai Career and Technology, Career Education, or applied technical-skills blueprint for ป.1 through ม.6.

## 1. Official-source-first authority check

Start with the Bureau of Academic Affairs and Educational Standards, OBEC, rather than a generic web search:

- **OBEC curriculum hub:** https://academic.obec.go.th/web/news/view/75
- **Basic Education Core Curriculum B.E. 2551, Thai:** https://drive.google.com/open?id=1mKyU6tkVWlL5b6vfwHNEzqkcqVXf_H-m
- **Legacy indicators: Career and Technology:** https://drive.google.com/open?id=0B9t56k6dmUe5em9mOEFXaU5vRjg
- **OBEC Order 921/2561:** https://drive.google.com/open?id=19xkmIK0bmefRl6uYd8B_9xkQ-oWZayI-

The OBEC hub is valuable because it exposes the document title and the direct file URL in one place. Read Order 921/2561 before describing the current status of a topic.

For technology and design, use the IPST source set:

- **IPST curriculum hub:** https://www.ipst.ac.th/curriculum
- **IPST Design and Technology hub:** https://www.ipst.ac.th/designtechnology
- **Project 14, M.1: Technology in occupations:** https://proj14.ipst.ac.th/m1/m1-dt/dt-m1b1-002/
- **Project 14, M.3: Using technology to solve occupational problems:** https://proj14.ipst.ac.th/m3/m3-dt/dt-m3b1-007/
- **Project 14, M.4: Technology impacts on people, society, economy, and environment:** https://proj14.ipst.ac.th/m4-6-dt/m4-dt/dt-m4b1-006/

## 2. Important curriculum-status distinction

The original B.E. 2551 Career and Technology document presents four strands:

1. การดำรงชีวิตและครอบครัว: Living and Family
2. การออกแบบและเทคโนโลยี: Design and Technology
3. เทคโนโลยีสารสนเทศและการสื่อสาร: Information and Communication Technology
4. การอาชีพ: Career

OBEC Order 921/2561 cancels the standards and indicators for strands 2 and 3 inside the former Career and Technology learning area and changes the learning-area name. In the revised arrangement, Design and Technology is handled under Science and Technology, Technology strand, through the IPST 2560 curriculum materials.

Therefore, do **not** claim that Woodwork, Metalwork, Electronics, or Design and Fabrication are four named national strands of the current Career learning area. They are best represented as:

- applied or enrichment modules under work-process and career competencies;
- school or local curriculum units;
- cross-curricular Design and Technology or Science and Technology projects; or
- bridges to vocational education and training.

Career Exploration, Entrepreneurship, Work Ethics, and Job Application Skills are competency clusters aligned mainly with the Career strand, especially occupational awareness, work habits, work processes, and pathways. They should still be mapped to explicit Thai curriculum language where possible, but should not be presented as separate national standards unless a current school or vocational curriculum is cited.

## 3. Topic mapping for a blueprint

| Topic | Primary curriculum anchor | Status label to use |
|---|---|---|
| Woodwork | ง 1.1 work process, materials, tools, household/product work; local or vocational extension | Applied/enrichment |
| Metalwork | ง 1.1 work process and tools; local or vocational extension | Applied/enrichment |
| Electronics | Science and Technology, Design and Technology, physical-science foundations, and project work | Cross-curricular/enrichment |
| Design and Fabrication | IPST Design and Technology and engineering-design practice | Cross-curricular core extension |
| Career Exploration | การอาชีพ and career guidance activities | Career competency |
| Entrepreneurship | การอาชีพ, value creation, enterprise and financial literacy extensions | Career competency/enrichment |
| Work Ethics | ง 1.1 work habits, responsibility, cooperation, resource and environmental consciousness; ง 4.1 attitude to work | Core competency |
| Job Application Skills | ง 4.1 occupational pathways, necessary skills, experience, attitude, and career preparation | Career competency |

This status table prevents a common error: treating a useful teaching sequence as if it were the official list of national strands.

## 4. Grade-band progression pattern

Use the four bands requested by the user, but distinguish exposure from independent operation:

| Band | Appropriate progression |
|---|---|
| ป.1-3 | Observe occupations; name common materials and tools; follow a short sequence; make simple paper, cardboard, recycled, clay, or pre-cut material products; practice tidiness, sharing, asking permission, and stopping when unsure. No independent cutting, drilling, soldering, welding, mains electricity, or powered machinery. |
| ป.4-6 | Measure and mark simple dimensions; select materials; use teacher-approved hand tools under direct supervision; assemble simple structures or low-voltage circuits; record steps, cost, quality, and waste; interview a worker or compare local occupations. Tools remain low-risk and age-appropriate. |
| ม.1-3 | Define a user need; write criteria and constraints; produce sketches or a simple CAD model; plan a process; prototype with safe hand tools, low-voltage electronics, or supervised fabrication; test, collect evidence, improve, and explain career connections. |
| ม.4-6 | Build a portfolio-quality project; compare material/process options; use CAD, measurement, documentation, costing, quality checks, and risk assessment; connect project outcomes to occupations, entrepreneurship, workplace ethics, and further study. Hazardous machinery, welding, mains work, and chemical finishing require qualified supervision, facility controls, and applicable regulations. |

## 5. Source hierarchy and research workflow

1. Read the OBEC curriculum hub and its linked order or PDF first.
2. Verify current curriculum status against the order, not only the title of a legacy PDF.
3. Use IPST curriculum pages and Project 14 to verify Design and Technology progression by grade.
4. Use professional safety authorities for workshop content:
   - OSHA Woodworking: https://www.osha.gov/woodworking
   - OSHA Woodworking eTool: https://www.osha.gov/etools/woodworking
   - OSHA Machine Guarding: https://www.osha.gov/machine-guarding
   - OSHA Electrical: https://www.osha.gov/electrical
   - OSHA Welding, Cutting, and Brazing: https://www.osha.gov/welding-cutting-brazing
   - HSE Woodworking publications: https://www.hse.gov.uk/PuBns/woodindx.htm
   - HSE Welding Fume: https://www.hse.gov.uk/welding/protect-your-workers/index.htm
5. Use engineering-education and design sources for process vocabulary:
   - ITEEA STEL: https://www.iteea.org/Publications/STEMProducts/177657.aspx
   - NGSS Engineering Design, 3-5: https://www.nextgenscience.org/dci-arrangement/3-5-ets1-engineering-design
   - NGSS Engineering Design, middle school: https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ets1-1-engineering-design
   - ISO 9001 overview: https://www.iso.org/standard/62085.html
6. Use official or intergovernmental career and enterprise sources:
   - Thailand Department of Employment: https://www.doe.go.th/
   - Thai มีงานทำ platform: https://ไทยมีงานทํา.doe.go.th/
   - Thai SME Coach: https://coach.sme.go.th/
   - EU JRC EntreComp: https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/entrecomp-entrepreneurship-competence-framework_en
   - UNESCO lifelong learning: https://www.unesco.org/en/lifelong-learning

Batch only a few discovery searches, then follow the authoritative URLs directly. Search snippets are useful for finding sources but are not sufficient evidence for curriculum claims. If a PDF's Thai text extracts as legacy-font gibberish, do not quote the corrupted extraction: use the official page title, the accessible order text, a better text layer, or a browser view and cite the source URL.

## 6. Blueprint quality gates

Every topic note should include:

- a one-sentence curriculum-status label: core, cross-curricular, or applied/enrichment;
- four grade-band outcomes, not merely a list of tools;
- key concepts and a process or professional-skill sequence;
- Thai terminology in parentheses after English terms;
- safety, ethics, inclusion, environmental responsibility, and supervision boundaries;
- one feasible project with an observable artifact and assessment evidence;
- sources separated into Thai curriculum authority, technical or safety authority, and career/industry authority.

For workshop topics, use a hierarchy of controls: eliminate or substitute hazards where possible, then engineering controls, administrative controls, and PPE. For younger learners, prefer observing, measuring, designing, and assembling over operating hazardous equipment. For electronics, keep school projects battery-powered or otherwise safety-extra-low-voltage and never imply that children should work on mains circuits.
