# STAR Question Bank — Behavioral Interview Mapping

**Purpose:** Map common behavioral questions to story dimensions for quick interview prep.

## Question Categories & Story Dimensions

### Leadership & Influence
- "Tell me about a time you led a project."
- "Describe a situation where you had to influence others without authority."
- "How do you handle coordinating a large team?"
- **Dimensions:** Technical leadership, team coordination, stakeholder communication

**Example stories:**
- CP All Migration (50+ person project, temporary team lead)
- Mentoring interns (teaching, delegation, feedback)

### Technical Judgment & Problem-Solving
- "Tell me about a complex technical problem you solved."
- "Describe a time you made a difficult architectural decision."
- "How do you approach legacy code or systems?"
- **Dimensions:** Technical depth, decision-making, learning agility

**Example stories:**
- CP All Migration (DB2→MySQL, stored procedure conversion, 90% test coverage)
- Agoda Observability (building monitoring from scratch, MTTD 24hr→1hr)

### Mentoring & Teaching
- "Tell me about a time you mentored someone."
- "How do you onboard new team members?"
- "Describe a time you gave difficult feedback."
- **Dimensions:** Teaching ability, empathy, adaptability, investment in others

**Example stories:**
- Mentoring 4 interns (PIM university students, Node 12→24 migration outcome)

### Ambiguity & Initiative
- "Tell me about a time you identified a problem and solved it proactively."
- "Describe a project with unclear requirements."
- "How do you handle situations where you don't have all the information?"
- **Dimensions:** Initiative, problem identification, self-direction

**Example stories:**
- Agoda Observability (identified monitoring gap, built solution as intern)
- Documentation creation for interns (proactively created onboarding materials)

### Failure & Learning
- "Tell me about a time you failed or made a mistake."
- "Describe a project that didn't go as planned."
- "What would you do differently if you could do it again?"
- **Dimensions:** Self-awareness, learning from mistakes, resilience

**Example stories:**
- (To be extracted — ask about projects that had setbacks or unexpected outcomes)

### Collaboration & Communication
- "Tell me about a time you worked with a difficult team member."
- "How do you communicate technical concepts to non-technical stakeholders?"
- "Describe a time you had to coordinate across teams."
- **Dimensions:** Cross-functional work, communication clarity, conflict resolution

**Example stories:**
- CP All Migration (service owner coordination, resource pool model)
- Mentoring interns (adapting to different learning styles, non-coding interns)

### Adaptability & Growth
- "Tell me about a time you had to learn something new quickly."
- "Describe a situation where you had to adapt your approach."
- "How do you handle feedback or criticism?"
- **Dimensions:** Learning agility, flexibility, openness to feedback

**Example stories:**
- CP All Migration (learning JasperSoft on-site, reverse-engineering stored procedures)
- Mentoring interns (adapting teaching style, practice project before real work)

## STAR Structure Checklist

For each story, ensure:

**Situation (30 seconds)**
- [ ] Specific context (company, project, timeframe)
- [ ] Clear problem or challenge
- [ ] Stakes (why it mattered)

**Task (15 seconds)**
- [ ] Your specific responsibility
- [ ] What success looked like
- [ ] Constraints or limitations

**Action (2 minutes — longest section)**
- [ ] Specific steps you took
- [ ] Technical details (tools, patterns, decisions)
- [ ] Collaboration or leadership moments
- [ ] Challenges you overcame

**Result (30 seconds)**
- [ ] Quantified outcomes (numbers, percentages, time saved)
- [ ] Business impact (if known)
- [ ] What you learned
- [ ] How it ended (handoff, go-live, next phase)

## Common Follow-Up Questions

Prepare for these probes on any story:

1. **"What would you do differently?"**
   - Shows learning and self-awareness
   - Don't be defensive — acknowledge what you'd improve

2. **"How did you balance this with other responsibilities?"**
   - Shows time management and prioritization
   - Be specific about time allocation

3. **"What was the biggest challenge?"**
   - Don't say "nothing was hard" — pick a real obstacle
   - Show how you overcame it

4. **"How did others react to your approach?"**
   - Shows awareness of team dynamics
   - Include both positive and constructive feedback you received

5. **"Why did you choose that specific technical approach?"**
   - Shows technical judgment
   - Explain trade-offs you considered

## Metric Framing Examples

| Raw Metric | Reframed for Impact |
|------------|---------------------|
| 95% uptime | "Processed 10K-20K daily transactions" (omit uptime if below 99.9%) |
| 1 hour → 3-5 minutes | "95% reduction in deployment time" |
| 0% → 90% test coverage | "Built test suite from scratch, achieved 90% coverage" |
| 24 hours → 1 hour MTTD | "96% reduction in mean time to detect incidents" |
| 15 hours → 2-3 hours | "85% reduction in manual processing time" |
| "Helped finish" | "Collaborated to complete X, specifically built Y component" |
| "Temporary lead" | "Led during critical migration phase" |

## Story Quality Checklist

Before using a story in an interview:

- [ ] Can I tell it in 3-4 minutes without rushing?
- [ ] Does it have specific, memorable details (not generic)?
- [ ] Are the metrics real and defensible?
- [ ] Can I answer "what would you do differently?" honestly?
- [ ] Does it showcase a strength relevant to the target role?
- [ ] Would the interviewer remember this story after 10 others?
